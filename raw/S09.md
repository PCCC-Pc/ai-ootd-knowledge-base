## Research purpose and method
The study examines the relationship between colour coordination and judgments of fashionableness. A total of 239 participants recruited through Amazon Mechanical Turk rated clothing colour combinations. Participants saw 30 combinations drawn from four palettes; the study analyzed women's and men's clothing separately.

## Findings
The results showed a quadratic relationship: the highest fashionableness ratings were associated with moderately coordinated combinations, rather than combinations that were extremely similar or extremely different. The authors discuss this as consistent with a Goldilocks principle balancing similarity and distinctiveness.

## Limitations and evidence boundary
The study focuses on judgments of selected colour combinations in controlled palettes. The authors note that fashion also depends on cut, design and trends, and recommend testing external validity in naturalistic settings such as magazines and runway shows. It does not provide a universal rule for all cultures, occasions, garment styles or individual preferences.
