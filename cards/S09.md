# Evidence Card S09

## APA 7th
Gray, K., Schmitt, P., Strohminger, N., & Kassam, K. S. (2014). The science of style: In fashion, colors should match only moderately. *PLOS ONE, 9*(7), e102772. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0102772

## Source quality
- Peer-reviewed open-access research article in *PLOS ONE*.
- Publisher full text and PubMed bibliographic record checked.
- Published 17 July 2014.

## Research question and method
The study tests how colour coordination relates to judgments of fashionableness. 239 participants recruited through Amazon Mechanical Turk rated 30 colour combinations drawn from four palettes. Women's and men's clothing were analyzed separately.

## Findings reported
The study found a quadratic relationship between colour coordination and fashionableness: moderately coordinated combinations received the highest fashionableness ratings. Both extremely similar and extremely different combinations were rated less favourably in the tested palettes.

## Wiki claims this card can support
1. In the study's tested palettes, perceived fashionableness was highest at a moderate level of colour coordination.
2. Outfit coordination need not mean making every item the same colour; contrast and similarity can be balanced.

## Limitations / boundaries
- Controlled colour-combination ratings are not the same as observing everyday outfits in natural settings.
- The authors note that cut, design and trendiness also matter and call for naturalistic validation.
- Do not present this finding as a universal colour formula or a replacement for individual preference and context.
