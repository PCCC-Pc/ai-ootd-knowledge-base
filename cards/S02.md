# Evidence Card S02

## APA 7th
Li, H.-C., Wang, L.-K., Chang, Y.-K., & Huang, K.-Y. (2025). Establishing colour harmony evaluation and recommendation model for clothing colour matching based on machine learning and deep learning. *Fashion and Textiles, 12*, Article 27. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40691-025-00433-y

## Source quality
- Research article published in *Fashion and Textiles*.
- Publisher full-text page and article metadata reviewed.
- Open Access; publisher page states CC BY 4.0.

## Research purpose
To study contemporary perceptions of clothing colour harmony and develop models for evaluating and recommending clothing colour combinations.

## Method / evidence
- Dataset: DeepFashion-Multimodal; the article reports 44,096 total images and 9,056 retained two-piece outfit images after filtering.
- Human evaluation: 20 observers evaluated 500 full-body clothing images.
- The article uses colour features and garment area proportions, and discusses rule-based models, SVM, custom CNN and GAN.

## Findings reported by the article
- Contemporary perceptions of clothing colour harmony differed from some traditional rules.
- The rule-based deep learning model reported macro accuracy of 0.98, F1 of 0.96 and R² of 0.92 for its regression task.
- The perception-based SVM and custom CNN models reported classification metrics above 0.99 and 0.95 respectively in the article's conclusion.
- The GAN-based model was used to generate clothing colour combinations based on the study's learned harmony perception.

## Limitations
The authors identify the small participant sample, limited coverage of racial/cultural differences and focus on two-colour clothing combinations as limitations. They recommend broader participant groups and validation under more varied real-world conditions.

## Wiki claims this card can support
1. Clothing colour harmony can be studied through human evaluation and image-based colour features.
2. ML/DL approaches can be developed for clothing colour-harmony evaluation and colour recommendation in the study's experimental setting.
3. Traditional colour-harmony rules should not automatically be treated as universal preferences.

## Claims outside the evidence
- This is not direct evidence for four-season personal-colour analysis.
- It does not prove a universal rule for matching an individual's skin tone.
- Its metrics are experimental results, not guaranteed performance in a deployed OOTD application.
